A perfect fit can tell
the wrong story.

With more features than observations, a regression can fit every training point and still predict well. Its coefficients may tell a much less reliable story about which features matter.

The central distinction

Right group. Wrong variables.

Suppose only two of eight features have an effect. If the features move together, spreading the same total effect across all eight can give similar predictions. It gives a different explanation.

Why does the prediction improve?

Prediction error is weighted by feature covariance. The redistribution preserves the group total, so its error lies entirely in within-group differences. Those differences have variance proportional to 1 − ρ.

Risk = (β̂ − β)′Σ(β̂ − β)
Signal R² = 1 − ¾ × (1 − ρ) / (1 + ρ)

Exact population calculation for the two coefficient vectors shown. This is an illustration of a preference of minimum-norm fitting, not a claim that the fitted estimator equals the spread vector. There is no observation noise in this example.

How the study creates the data

Many features, a few shared directions.

Every outcome is a linear signal plus independent Gaussian noise. Every feature mixes a shared group factor with independent variation of its own.

y = Xβ + ε
xⱼ = √ρ · zg(j) + √(1 − ρ) · uⱼ

z: shared factor · u: feature-specific variation · β: true effects · ε: outcome noise. All features have variance one.

Spikes & tail

Spikes: common movement within groups. A few strongly correlated groups create a few high-variance directions.

Tail: differences within each group. These directions have variance 1 − ρ.

For a group of m features, the shared direction has eigenvalue 1 − ρ + ρm. With independent features (ρ = 0), every direction has equal variance.

Where are the true effects?

  • Sparse: ten active features scattered at random.
  • Grouped: ten active features packed into as few groups as possible.
  • Dense: all features active.

Do their signs reinforce or cancel?

Aligned: positive effects reinforce the shared direction.

Contrast: signs alternate within groups. Even active counts cancel the shared loading; odd counts leave some behind.

We rescale β so that signal variance is always one. Signal-to-noise ratios of 1, 4 and 16 therefore mean noise variances of 1, ¼ and ¹⁄₁₆.

What is the minimum-norm estimator?
β̂ = X⁺y   =   arg min ‖b‖²   subject to Xb = y

Once X has full row rank, many coefficient vectors can fit all training observations. Minimum-norm regression chooses the shortest vector. Spreading an effect across correlated features can lower its norm.

We compare it with cross-validated ridge (shrink coefficients), lasso (select and shrink), post-lasso (select, then refit), and an oracle that knows the active features. For dense signals, knowing the active set does not remove the underdetermination.

Why can the scores disagree?

Prediction weights errors by Σ, the covariance of the features. Coefficient cosine weights feature directions equally.

Prediction: δ′Σδ
Coefficient direction: angle(β̂, β)

δ = β̂ − β. With correlated features, an incorrect allocation inside a group can matter little for prediction.

Benign does not mean useful.

As p grows beyond n, fitting noise can become less costly. But the remaining bias can still be large. The estimator needs the true signal to lie in directions it learns.

Group totals can be the right level.

Minimum-norm fitting tends to spread effects within correlated groups. That can recover a group’s role while obscuring which members caused the outcome in this model.

A small standard error can miss the real uncertainty.

A fitted coefficient mixes its own attenuated effect with leakage from other true effects. Scaling uncertainty only by observation noise ignores that leakage.

What the experiment supports

Choose the level of the claim.

For prediction: few strong factors help when the signal reinforces them. More features can reduce the harm from fitting noise without recovering signal hidden in other directions.

For interpretation: good predictions do not establish that individual coefficients are right. Factor totals can be more stable; individual effects need stronger justification. Lasso is a useful alternative when sparsity is credible.

For inference: leakage matters. A test can look precise while using the wrong uncertainty scale.

Design, definitions & limits

The grid has 3 feature-to-observation ratios × 5 covariance structures × 3 active-set patterns × 2 sign patterns × 3 SNR levels = 270 scenarios. Each uses 20 seeds. n = 100; ten effects are active unless the signal is dense.

Features and noise are Gaussian. Groups have equal sizes, active effects have equal absolute size, and signal variance is normalised to one. These results do not establish performance for arbitrary real-world data or identify causal effects outside this data-generating model.

Signal R² is 1 minus population excess risk, not conventional R² for the noisy outcome. Coefficient cosines measure direction, not magnitude. Prediction risk is computed exactly using the known population covariance, rather than estimated on a finite test set.
